- **[A-06]** `make` is not installed on this machine, so `run.py` mirrors every Makefile target and is what I actually executed. Both are committed; the Makefile stays the documented interface for graders on Linux/macOS. — Nothing; the two are kept in sync by hand and the target lists are short.
- **[A-07]** The resolution-bearing scorer went through three measured versions before I stopped: **v1 12.2%** (bug: every Spotify link is a `t.co` shortlink and my patterns only matched `<URL>`/`spotify.com/support`, so the support-link signal never fired on raw text) -> **v2 58.5%** (over-permissive: "can you DM us your account's email" is a handoff but matched no handoff pattern) -> **v3 36.4%** (final). I stopped tuning and instead *measured* the filter (see [A-08]) rather than iterating until I liked the number. — A validation result showing systematically biased errors.
- **[A-08]** The resolution filter is validated on a **random sample of 100** replies from the population it runs on, adjudicated by `gpt-4.1-mini` against a written definition: **precision 0.806, recall 0.853, F1 0.829, Cohen's kappa 0.736**, heuristic positive rate 0.36 vs adjudicator 0.34. The adjudicator is a MODEL, not a human -- this is machine-vs-machine agreement and is labelled as such everywhere it appears. — A human pass over the same 100 disagreeing materially with the adjudicator.
- **[A-09]** Thread reconstruction grows the brand's conversational neighbourhood with 6 iterative vectorised `isin` hops rather than indexing all 2.81M rows, which would cost several GB of dicts. 91,833 rows are threaded instead of 2.81M, giving the same threads at a fraction of the memory. — Evidence that real SpotifyCares threads exceed 6 hops from a brand tweet.
- **[A-10]** The English filter needed a second gate. Script-detection alone passed an entire induced cluster of **romanised Indonesian** ("min sy mau beli spotify premium, bisa pake kartu debit gak??"), which is pure Latin script. Added an English-function-word requirement; measured it, found 3 false positives in the first sample ("Y'all being DDoS'd?", "we need full catalogue", "where's Taylor Swifts new album"), expanded the word list and added one-rule de-suffixing (`where's`->`wheres`->`where`). Final drop rate **426/10,284 = 4.1%** of resolution-bearing threads, spot-checked as genuinely Indonesian/French/Polish/Turkish/Swedish/Czech/Tagalog. Chosen over adding `langdetect` because it stays explainable and langdetect is unreliable on <10-token tweets anyway. — Evidence that it drops a meaningful share of genuine English messages.
- **[A-11]** The taxonomy is **weakly** induced, and I say so rather than implying otherwise. HDBSCAN produced 0 clusters / 100% noise; KMeans silhouette was ~0.04 at every k in 6..20 (no natural structure in short support tweets); silhouette argmax was k=7 but I chose k=9 as the best value inside the 8-14 target band. Clustering isolated only **5 of 11** intents; the other 6 are evidenced by keyword probes + reading (`scripts/intent_evidence.py`) and are marked "no cluster support" in `reports/figures/taxonomy.png`. — Stronger embeddings (e5/bge) or clustering brand replies instead of customer messages might find real structure; that is in the one-more-week list.
- **[A-12]** `billing_charge_dispute` has only **3 keyword matches in 6,791 corpus messages**. Billing is handled almost entirely in DM, so it is near-absent from public data. I keep the intent (it is the highest-stakes escalation class and it does occur) but expect it to fall below the 12-example golden floor, and per-class metrics for it are reported with raw support counts and explicitly refused as evidence. — A looser probe or the LLM prelabel pass finding materially more than regex does.
- **[A-13]** **Correction to [A-12]:** my regex probe found only 3 `billing_charge_dispute` messages in the corpus, but the LLM pass over the test split found **19 (1.3%)** and the golden set now holds **12** — enough to meet the floor. The regex was far too narrow, not the data. Billing is still the rarest intent and still under-represented because it moves to DM, but the "3 in 6,791" figure was an artefact of my probe and is not repeated in the report.
- **[A-14]** **A real design bug, found and fixed.** The golden set was first stratified using a cheap intent-only prompt while the labels came from the richer Pass A prompt. The two disagreed enough that a sample built to hold ≥12 per intent ended up with 4/6/8 for three intents once Pass A relabelled it. Fixed by running **Pass A over the whole test split** (1,456 rows, ~$0.40) and stratifying on the same procedure that produces the final labels; Pass A on the 220 is now a projection of that run, so the sampling labels and the golden labels are literally identical. All 11 intents now meet the floor. — Nothing; stratifying on different labels than you grade with is simply wrong.
- **[A-15]** Pass A and Pass B disagree on escalation far more than their kappa alone suggests: **A escalates 15.0% of messages, B escalates 22.3%** on the same 220, kappa 0.70. The two prompts differ only in framing (A: intent-then-escalate; B: escalate-first, as a senior reviewer). A 7-point swing from framing alone means my escalation boundary is genuinely ambiguous, not that one prompt is broken. This is a headline item in the misleading-numbers section. — A human adjudication pass showing one framing is consistently right.
